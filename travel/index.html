TRAVELLING

A trip isn't a diet break and it isn't a free-for-all either. Here's how to stay roughly on track without stressing about it.

Message me before you go — if your program needs adjusting around your trip, we'll sort it in advance.

TRAINING WHILE AWAY
  • No gym access? A simple bodyweight session (squats, push-ups, lunges, planks) is enough to maintain, not build — that's fine for a short trip.
  • Many hotels have a gym or a day-pass option nearby — worth a quick check when you book.
  • Missing a session or two isn't going to undo your progress. Consistency over months matters far more than any single trip.
NUTRITION WHILE AWAY
  • Same principles as eating out apply — protein-forward choices where you can, don't overthink the rest.
  • Prioritise protein at each meal first, then fill in around it.
  • Stay on top of water — it's easy to under-drink when you're out of routine, especially flying.
  • Aim for "roughly on track," not perfect. Perfect isn't realistic on a trip, and it's not the goal.
STAY ACTIVE

Travelling often means more walking than usual — sightseeing, exploring, getting around. That counts. You don't need to manufacture extra activity on top of a full day on your feet.

WHEN YOU'RE BACK

Jump straight back into your normal routine — no need for extreme measures to "make up" for time away. One trip doesn't undo your progress, and treating it like it does is how people spiral. Get back to your plan and keep moving forward.
