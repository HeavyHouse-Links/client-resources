RECOVERY

Training is where you break the body down — recovery is where it rebuilds. You don't grow in the gym, you grow between sessions. The good news: most of what works is free, and none of it is complicated.
SLEEP — THE BIG ONE
Aim for 7–9 hours, most nights. Not every night — most nights. One bad night changes nothing. A bad fortnight changes everything: strength, appetite, mood and motivation all slide together.
Keep roughly the same sleep and wake times. Your body runs on rhythm, not catch-up. The weekend sleep-in helps a little, but it never fully squares the debt — consistency does.
Cool, dark, boring room. Drop the temperature, kill the light, and put the phone on charge across the room. The most effective sleep tool on the market is a charger you can't reach from bed.
Mind the caffeine cutoff. Caffeine hangs around in your system for hours. If sleep is a struggle, make your last coffee 8+ hours before bed and give it two weeks before you judge the result.
FOOD & FLUIDS
Protein at every meal. Muscle repairs with protein, and it works best spread across the day — not saved up for one giant dinner. Your plan sets the target; this is about hitting it consistently.
Eat properly on rest days too. Rest days are when the rebuilding actually happens. Cutting food hard on your day off starves the exact process you're resting for.
Carbs are the refill, not the enemy. Training empties the tank and carbs fill it back up. Constantly training on empty doesn't make you leaner — it just makes every session feel worse.
Water all day, not all at once. Even mild dehydration makes training feel harder than it should. Easy check: if your urine is pale yellow, you're doing fine.
REST DAYS
Rest days are programmed, not optional. They're in your plan on purpose — they ARE the plan. Training through them isn't extra credit, it's borrowing from next week and paying interest.
Movement is medicine. A rest day isn't a couch day. A walk, a swim, an easy bike — gentle movement gets blood to sore muscles and beats total stillness every time.
Rest without guilt. If you feel lazy on a rest day, read the first line of this page again. The work only turns into results while you're recovering from it.
SORENESS
Soreness is not the scoreboard. Sore doesn't mean the session worked, and fresh doesn't mean it didn't. Progress shows up in your numbers and photos — not in how much your legs hate stairs.
The best fix for soreness is easy movement. Blood flow is what clears it. A walk and some light activity will do more than a day of sitting perfectly still ever will.
Know sore from pain. A dull ache across the whole muscle is normal. Sharp, one-sided, or inside a joint is not — message me before you train through that one.
THE EXTRAS — WORTH IT OR NOT

Honest rule: the free stuff — sleep, food, walking, water — does 95% of the job. Everything below is the last 5%.

Ice baths: timing matters. Great for feeling human again, but taken straight after lifting they can blunt some of the muscle-building signal. Keep them well away from strength sessions.
Massage guns, foam rollers, stretching: nice, not magic. If it feels good, use it — it may help you move and relax. Just know you can't buy what sleep gives you for free.
Saunas and hot baths: relax with them. Good for winding down and easing stiffness, and anything that helps you sleep earns its keep. Treat them as a sleep aid, not a shortcut.
WHEN TO PUT YOUR HAND UP
The warning signs of under-recovery: weights that moved fine last week feel glued to the floor, sleep gets worse instead of better, you're snapping at people, and you're dreading sessions you normally enjoy. That's not weakness — that's data.
That's a message-me moment. Your plan is built around your busiest week, not your best one — so when life load goes up, the plan flexes with it. One message and we adjust. Grinding through is how a flat week becomes a flat month.
