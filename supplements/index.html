SUPPLEMENTS — WORTH IT OR NOT

The free stuff — sleep, food, water, training — does 95% of the job. Supplements are the last 5%, and most of the shelf is marketing. Here's the honest sort.
WORTH IT
Creatine monohydrate. The most proven supplement in existence — more strength, more repeat efforts, and it costs cents a day. 3–5g every day, timing doesn't matter, no loading phase needed. Buy plain monohydrate and never pay extra for "HCL", blends or fancy branding — they do nothing more.
Whey protein. Not magic — just convenient food. A scoop is 25g of protein for about 130 calories, which makes hitting your daily target dramatically easier. If you already hit your protein from food, you don't need it.
Caffeine. The most proven performance enhancer there is, and coffee is the cheap form. A coffee before training does what most $70 pre-workouts do. The catch is sleep — keep it away from evenings (see the Recovery guide).
DEPENDS ON YOU
Electrolytes. Earn their keep in long, hot, sweaty sessions and physical outdoor work — FIFO site swings in summer are exactly the use case. For a normal hour in an air-conditioned gym, water and salted food cover it.
Magnesium. May help sleep and cramps if your intake is genuinely low. Food covers most people — nuts, greens, whole grains — but it's cheap and low-risk if you want to trial it for sleep.
Vitamin D. Relevant if you barely see the sun — night shift, indoor work, winter. Don't guess: it's one blood test, so ask your GP before you buy anything.
SAVE YOUR MONEY
Fat burners. No supplement burns fat. A calorie deficit does. Every "thermogenic" on the shelf is caffeine in a costume with a markup.
BCAAs. If you eat enough protein, you already have them — whey and every protein food is full of them. Buying them separately is buying something twice.
Greens powders. Expensive vegetable dust. Buy actual vegetables — they're cheaper and do more. If you genuinely won't eat any, that's a habit to fix, not a powder to buy.
Detox teas and cleanses. Your liver and kidneys are the detox, and they're already working. These products are laxatives with marketing budgets.
Test boosters. The herbal ones do nothing measurable. The things that actually support your hormones are free: sleep, food, training and managing stress.
Heavy House  ·  General guidance, not medical advice. Check with your GP before starting any supplement, especially if you're on medication, pregnant or managing a condition.
